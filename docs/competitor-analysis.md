## What each one does best, and what's missing

**Wizcraft**
- Best: heavy credibility, with "since 1988", Commonwealth Games and Hockey World Cup ceremonies, and offices in five cities.
- Missing: the homepage is mostly long paragraphs. There's no showreel, no visible enquiry button, and no phone or WhatsApp link. An insolvency (CIRP) notice on the homepage hurts trust.

**E-Factor**
- Best: clear verticals (Government, Weddings, Sporting, Festivals, Eco-habitats), a "global footprint" section, named leadership with photos, and Awards and Press pages.
- Missing: the hero is a photo slider with a caption only. There's no headline saying what they do, and no button. The page is very long (~16,000 px) with several "Load more" buttons. There's no phone, WhatsApp or video on the homepage.

**DreamzKrraft (the best in this set)**
- Best: the hero says who they are and backs it up ("India's most awarded luxury wedding planners") with a "Schedule a consultation" button right there. Case studies name real couples. It has "75+ awards", a "Featured on" press strip, a consultation form, and WhatsApp and phone links.
- Missing: weddings only, so a corporate client has no path. The page is extremely long (~20,000 px).

**Tamarind Global**
- Best: three clear "doors" (Holidays, Celebrations, Corporate), a proof strip (20+ years, 400+, 1M+), and a values section.
- Missing: the imagery feels like stock, the headline is generic ("Unforgettable experiences"), there are no buttons, and there's no WhatsApp or phone link.

**Shaadi Squad**
- Missing: the homepage is essentially an Instagram feed, with no services, proof, form or contact path. This is what to avoid.

**Moment Factory**
- Best: work comes first, with featured projects grouped by type (concerts, brand activations, public spaces). Short press quotes act as proof, and there's plenty of video.
- Missing: no quick enquiry path, and the site is heavy.

**Production Club**
- Best: every project is titled as client + event ("KAROL G – NFL São Paulo Halftime"), so the work proves itself. Big, image-led scrolling.
- Missing: very long (~31,000 px), with no planning tool and no clear way to start a project from the homepage.

## Patterns that win

1. **The hero says who you are, proves it, and has one clear button.** Only DreamzKrraft does all three.
2. **Named work beats generic galleries.** Each project should show the client or event type, the city and the scale.
3. **Proof sits high on the page:** client logos, numbers, awards and press.
4. **Separate doors for different audiences.** Corporate buyers and families want different things.
5. **Fast contact:** WhatsApp and phone. Only 1 of the 7 had both on the homepage.

## Gaps nobody fills, and AARMBH's opening

| Gap across competitors | What AARMBH does instead |
|---|---|
| No site serves corporate and families well in one place | The hero has two doors, "For brands" and "For families", and the services section switches between them |
| 6 of 7 have no WhatsApp or phone link on the homepage | WhatsApp in the hero, the planner, the form and the mobile bar |
| None lets you plan or estimate online | Keep and sharpen the 30-second planner, which sends to WhatsApp |
| Little transparency on how they work | A 5-step process with rough timings, plus an FAQ |
| No FAQ (budgets, travel, lead time, own equipment) | A new FAQ section, which also helps Google search |
| Pages of 10,000–31,000 px that tire people on phones | A tighter page: work and gallery merged into one section, no duplicate sections |
| Text-only heroes or photo sliders with no message | A clear headline, proof, CTA and the showreel in the first screen |

## What changed in the AARMBH redesign

1. **Hero:** removed the giant three-line headline and the particle sphere, which competed with the photo. It now has a clear headline and subline, two buttons (Plan your event / WhatsApp), a proof row, the showreel in a framed "stage" card, and two audience doors.
2. **Client strip** moved directly under the hero.
3. **Selected work:** a filterable set of case-study cards (type, city, guests, build time, what we did) replaces both the "Worlds" carousel and the 3D gallery.
4. **Services:** a "For brands / For families" switch, plus a list of in-house capabilities. This replaces the 3D carousel, which looked good but said little.
5. **Showreel**, kept, with a new title.
6. **Why AARMBH:** the scroll-lit manifesto, four differentiators and the stats in one section.
7. **Process** with timings, **Planner** kept, **Testimonials** kept, **FAQ** new, **Brief form** kept, **Footer** updated.

## Content the client must supply or confirm before launch

- Real event photos and the real phone number (currently `+91 99999 99999`).
- **Case-study details:** the six work cards reuse the sample captions already on the site (Udaipur wedding, Goa festival, and so on). Replace them with real projects.
- **Client names** in the logo strip (Red Bull, BMW, Tata Motors…) and the **testimonials** are placeholders from the earlier build. Only use names you have permission to show.
- **Stats** (250+ productions, 32+ cities, 500k+ guests) and **FAQ answers** (lead times, travel, payment terms) need confirming.

# Design direction v3: "Stagecraft Editorial"

Feedback on v2: the name is **AARMBH**, the hero is boring, the pill shapes have to go, the site needs structure and a design philosophy, better fonts and alignment, multiple themes, small interactions, 3D, and transitions. It should feel minimal, fashionable and classy.

What award-winning sites did in 2026 (Awwwards/FWA commentary, quiet-luxury and editorial trend write-ups):
- **Restraint wins.** Custom shaders and scroll-driven storytelling are in. Generic particle backgrounds are out.
- **Type leads.** One display face and one workhorse sans, with the largest heading at least 6× the smallest text. Oversized display serifs in the hero.
- **Quiet luxury.** Generous whitespace, muted neutrals, hairline rules, a refined serif, restrained motion, and premium photography.
- **Magazine on the outside, app on the inside.** Deliberate pacing, fast interactions, and respect for reduced motion.

## The system

| Layer | Decision |
|---|---|
| Grid | 12 columns, 24 px gutters, 1440 px max width. Every block starts and ends on a column line, and sections are separated by hairline rules |
| Shape | Square corners. No pills or rounded buttons. Buttons are outlined rectangles that fill from below on hover |
| Type | **Fraunces** (variable serif, light weight, italic for emphasis) for display. **Inter Tight** for text and UI. **JetBrains Mono** for indexes, labels and data |
| Colour | One accent per theme. No neon glows |
| Themes | **Noir** (black + champagne), **Ivory** (paper + oxblood), **Aurora** (the original midnight gradient), **Marigold** (maroon + marigold, festive). Switching themes uses a circular reveal and remembers your choice |
| 3D | The hero is a WebGL image plane in perspective. It tilts toward the cursor, ripples where you point, splits colour when you move fast, and dissolves between event types with a noise transition. Work rows show a floating image that leans in 3D with cursor speed |
| Motion | Line-masked text reveals, a curtain preloader, scroll-lit manifesto words, the showreel opening from an inset frame, magnetic links and a blend-mode cursor. All of it is off under reduced motion |

# Global hero benchmark (27 Oct 2026)

| Studio | Hero |
|---|---|
| Jack Morton (global experiential) | Full-screen looping video of real work, a 3-word headline ("Experience Without Limits"), a round **Watch reel** button |
| Colin Cowie (luxury weddings & events) | Full-screen film, one line ("40 years of extraordinary"), **Event inquiry** in the nav |
| GPJ (experience marketing) | Full-screen video, no text at all |
| Balich Wonder Studio (Olympic ceremonies) | Full-screen carousel of iconic event photos, only arrows and dots |
| Moment Factory, Production Club | The work comes first; every project is named by client + event |

**The pattern:** real work in motion, full screen, very little text, one way to watch the reel and one way to enquire. Nobody uses distortion effects or giant wordmarks.

**What AARMBH does now:** a full-screen photo crossfade with a slow drift, a two-line headline, a round **Watch reel** button that opens a full-screen player with sound, and **Plan an event**.
**Next step for the client:** supply a clean 15–30 s landscape loop of real events (1080p, no borders or frames). It replaces the photo slideshow as a background video, exactly like Jack Morton and GPJ.
